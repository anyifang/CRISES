## Overview

**CRISES** is a spatially explicit modelling framework developed to assess the climate resilience of expanding variable renewable energy (VRE) systems under intensifying tropical-cyclone (TC) hazards.  
It links:

- **physics-based TC hazard projection**
- **power-system optimization**
- **technology-specific damage and downtime assessment**
- **economic loss analysis and adaptation planning**

CRISES translates projected TC climatology changes into **engineering consequences**, **generation losses**, **economic losses**, and **economically optimal adaptation strategies** for future VRE deployment.

---

## Why CRISES?

The global energy transition increasingly depends on large-scale wind and solar deployment in coastal regions.  
These same regions are often highly exposed to tropical cyclones.

CRISES is designed to bridge the scale gap between:

- **climate-hazard projection**, and
- **policy-relevant energy-system planning**

By coupling hazard, exposure, vulnerability, and adaptation within one framework, CRISES enables risk-informed planning for climate-resilient renewable-energy systems.

---

## Core capabilities

### 1. Tropical-cyclone hazard projection
CRISES uses a physics-based downscaling framework to generate synthetic tropical cyclones under historical and future climate conditions, and converts them into high-resolution wind fields through an analytical TC structure model.

### 2. Spatially resolved power-system planning
CRISES incorporates a **0.25° power-system optimization module** to represent where future onshore wind, offshore wind, distributed PV (DPV), and utility-scale PV (UPV) are likely to be deployed.

### 3. Infrastructure downtime and damage assessment
CRISES quantifies both:

- **temporary interruption**  
  (for example, turbine cut-out and irradiance attenuation)

- **permanent damage**  
  (for example, tower yielding, buckling, and structural PV failure)

### 4. Economic loss and adaptation analysis
CRISES converts TC-induced infrastructure impacts into:

- downtime
- equivalent capacity loss
- power-generation loss
- direct and indirect economic loss

It further identifies **economically optimal design wind speeds** through cost–benefit analysis, providing actionable guidance for resilient infrastructure planning.
